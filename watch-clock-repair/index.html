Care for Antique Timepieces

Antique Watch & Clock Repair

Discuss the condition and repair needs of an antique watch or clock with an experienced horological professional.

Begin With the Object in Front of You

Antique watches and clocks vary widely in age, construction, condition, and completeness. Before any work is considered, it is important to identify the piece, understand the problem, and recognize the historical information preserved in its components and surfaces.

Howard Gitman has been buying, selling, repairing, and appraising antique watches and clocks for over forty years. Contact him to describe the timepiece and discuss an appropriate next step.

What to Describe

Useful Information for a Repair Inquiry

The Timepiece

Identify whether it is a pocket watch, wristwatch, clock, movement, or another mechanical object.

The Condition

Describe what happens when it is wound or set, whether parts are loose, and any visible damage.

The History

Share what is known about prior work, missing components, family history, storage, and accompanying papers.

Before Attempting to Make It Run

  • Do not force the winding mechanism. Resistance can indicate that the movement needs attention.
  • Do not apply household oil or cleaner. Materials not intended for horological mechanisms can create additional problems.
  • Do not polish away original surfaces. Patina, marks, and finishes can be part of the object’s history.
  • Keep loose parts together. Save hands, crystals, keys, screws, papers, and other associated pieces.
