Understanding What You Have

An antique watch may carry several kinds of value: as a working instrument, a piece of craftsmanship, a historical object, a family heirloom, or a collectible. An informed appraisal begins by identifying the object and understanding its condition and context.

Howard Gitman has worked with antique watches and clocks for over forty years and has been a member of the National Association of Watch and Clock Collectors since 1971.

Preparing an Inquiry

Information That Helps

01

Describe the Object

Share the type of item, maker name, inscriptions, approximate size, and what you know of its history.

02

Show the Condition

Photographs of the dial, case, back, movement, marks, and any damage can provide useful context.

03

Explain Your Goal

Say whether you are learning about an heirloom, considering a sale, or evaluating a collection.

Preserve the Item as Found

Avoid polishing, cleaning, forcing a case open, or winding a mechanism that resists. Original surfaces, marks, components, and accompanying papers can contain information useful to understanding a piece.

If you are unsure what to photograph or describe, begin with a general inquiry. Howard can explain what additional details may be helpful.
