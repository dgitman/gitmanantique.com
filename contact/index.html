Worldwide Inquiries

Gitman Antique Watches & Rarities serves collectors and owners around the world.

What to Include in an Email

  • What the item is: pocket watch, wristwatch, clock, movement, tool, enamel, music box, or collection.
  • Visible information: maker names, numbers, inscriptions, hallmarks, and approximate dimensions.
  • Current condition: whether it runs, what appears missing or damaged, and what you know about prior work.
  • Available history: how it was acquired, family or collection history, boxes, papers, and related material.
  • Your purpose: appraisal, possible sale, repair question, purchase inquiry, or general identification.

Photographs That May Help

01

Whole Object

Include front, back, sides, dial, case, and any accessories in even light.

02

Close Details

Photograph names, serial numbers, hallmarks, engravings, damage, and visible mechanisms.

03

Related Material

Include boxes, papers, chains, keys, tools, spare parts, and notes when available.

Not Sure Where to Begin?

You do not need to identify the maker, date, or value before getting in touch. A short description and a few clear photographs are enough to begin a conversation. Avoid forcing a case open, winding a resistant mechanism, or polishing the item first.
